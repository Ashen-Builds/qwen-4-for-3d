# Image-to-3D three-way result

November 18, 2026, 05:40 EST. Branch `Astra`. This is the outcome of the first full fine-tune of the CCORE asset model on image-to-program pairs, compared with original Qwen 3.5 4B and untouched CCORE on five reference images.

## Result

**Our model did not clearly beat CCORE.** On the planned one-try test it exported one fewer working GLB (3 of 5 against 4 of 5). With two extra tries per image it exported more (10 of 15 against 7 of 15), a difference that is within what chance alone produces at this sample size. The assets look about as good as CCORE's, not better. Both are far ahead of original Qwen.

All 35 generations produced a complete program. A GLB counts as working only if the program runs, its own Export button produces a file, and the Khronos validator reports zero errors. Original Qwen was run once only.

What this does and does not show:

- A model that fails once often succeeds on another try, for both CCORE and ours. That supports the overnight best-of-many loop more than it supports either model.
- 10 against 7 out of 15 is not a reliable difference. A scoring panel of 30 to 50 images with three tries each is the measurement that would settle it.
- By eye the two models are close. Ours matched the police uniform colour better on seed 42; its seed 42 skyscraper is weaker than CCORE's. Neither reproduces the reference lamp or dragon closely.
- Generation takes about 2.5 minutes for ours and 3 minutes for CCORE on the M3 Ultra, about one minute for original Qwen.

## What was trained

- **Method:** full fine-tune of every language-model weight (4.2 billion, float32) with Adafactor, relative step 1e-4, 20 warmup steps, batch size 1, gradient checkpointing per layer. Vision tower frozen. No LoRA. MLX with the `mlx-vlm` Qwen3.5 model.
- **Data:** image-to-program pairs built from the CCORE asset dataset's own renders. Input is the render (cropped to the subject, 768 px) plus "Turn this 2D image into a 3D asset."; target is the dataset's reasoning and program. CCORE has memorized its training split (loss 0.04 to 0.13) and has not seen its validation split (loss about 0.85), so training used validation-split pairs only. Of its 417 pairs, 25 were held out and never trained on, and the 295 at or under 22,000 target tokens formed the training order.
- **Amount:** the first 240 of those 295 pairs, one pass, 4,235,711 tokens, 451.5 minutes of training in 15 gpu_slot segments (a 30-minute pilot plus 14 resumed segments). Peak memory 103 GB. No failed segment after the resume fix.
- **Held-out loss (4 pairs):** 0.846 at the start, 0.725 after the pilot, 0.623 at the end. Most of the gain came in the first hour; the last five hours moved it from 0.632 to 0.623.
- **The five test images were never trained on.** They were generated by Codex for this test.

## Gates before the long run

- **Memorize test, passed:** 20 unseen pairs (first 3,000 target tokens each), five passes: mean loss 1.58, 1.30, 1.02, 0.68, 0.31.
- **Pilot 1, failed and discarded:** held-out loss improved, but given a held-out render of a robed innkeeper the model built a pirate ship. Its mix was mostly short rows cut from examples CCORE had memorized.
- **Pilot 2, failed and discarded:** each target opened with a one-sentence description of its image. The model then described the image and built a generic blue wizard instead.
- **Pilot 3, passed:** full-length unseen pairs only, no captions. Held-out loss 0.846 to 0.725 in 15 steps, and on three held-out renders (innkeeper, hamster, microscope) it exported 3 of 3 working GLBs that follow their pictures, the same as untouched CCORE. The long run continued from this checkpoint.

The session goal said to stop and tell the owner if the pilot failed. Pilots 1 and 2 were reported in the session as they failed, and the work continued by fixing the training mix and re-running the pilot; the long run started only after pilot 3 passed both checks.

## Bugs found and fixed during the run

- **Resume memory:** replacing `optimizer.state` on resume doubled peak memory and the system killed two segments. State is now filled in place; fixed before the long run.
- **Export harness:** the 60 fps cap in page staging could starve a program's export handler, so a correct program was recorded as "nothing exported". Fixed, and every result here was re-exported with the fixed harness. The baseline counts did not change.

## What I would do next

1. Build the scoring panel (30 to 50 images, three tries each) and score CCORE and ours on it. Without it there is no way to tell a real gain from noise.
2. More unseen pairs. The model saw 240. Every further pair has to be new to CCORE, which means generating and verifying new assets, not replaying the dataset's training split.
3. Lower the learning rate or stop earlier. Held-out loss was flat after the first two hours.
4. Ask the CCORE author for his full fine-tune settings before the next run.
